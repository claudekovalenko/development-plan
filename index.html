Data protection and reliability

The platform holds personal details and spiritual responses, so access is restricted by role. Each creator sees only their own contacts and responses. Responses combined across all creators are visible only to authorized Digital Collective admins, and that rule is enforced in the database itself, not just hidden in the screens. No creator can reach another creator’s data or the collective totals behind it.

To be agreed in writing before launch

  • Why each piece of data is collected and how it is used
  • How long records are kept
  • What happens when a creator or visitor asks for their account or data to be deleted
  • How long backups are kept, and how deletions apply to them
  • How a data breach is handled, and who notifies the people affected
  • Which analytics or other third-party services receive any data, and what they receive

Situations the MVP must handle

  • A visitor submits the same form twice: one contact, every response kept, no duplicate follow-up
  • A message would go out twice, for example after a retry: it is sent once
  • A submission times out or the connection drops: nothing is lost or saved twice, and the visitor sees what happened
  • A person unsubscribes: no other workflow can email them afterwards
  • A message lands in spam or fails to send: the failure or bounce is logged and visible to admins
  • The sending domain isn’t set up correctly: email stays off until the domain is verified

Launch readiness checklist

Before launch, each of these is tested and confirmed, so the risks above are checked rather than assumed.

Show the 16 checks

Access control

  • Creator A cannot view Creator B’s contacts or responses.
  • Creator A cannot access admin pages or admin APIs.
  • Direct URL and API requests enforce the same permissions as the interface.
  • CSV export cannot bypass creator-level restrictions.

Forms and data

  • Invalid inputs and oversized submissions are rejected safely.
  • Spam protection works without blocking legitimate users unnecessarily.
  • Repeated submissions do not create unintended duplicate follow-up emails.
  • Consent records capture the relevant wording/version, timestamp, and action.

Email

  • Unsubscribed addresses are suppressed across every sending path.
  • Email failures and bounces are recorded and handled.
  • Retries do not accidentally send duplicate emails.
  • Creator-specific links and content are sent to the correct recipients.

Deployment and recovery

  • The public site and creator pages work on iPhone, Android, and desktop.
  • A missing page, invalid creator slug, or expired link has a graceful response.
  • Secrets and service-role credentials are not exposed in browser code or public repository files.
  • Backups can be restored and deletion/retention behavior is documented.

Legal and compliance

The platform will collect names, emails, phone numbers and people’s spiritual responses, and will send email on the collective’s behalf. That brings in privacy, consent, email and accessibility rules. The developer builds the features that support compliance (consent records with version and time, unsubscribe and suppression, data export and deletion, access controls). The developer is not providing legal advice, and that does not replace the developer’s own contractual or technical obligations.

Email follow-up · protections for everyone involved

Follow-up email is a core part of the MVP. It carries less risk than SMS, because it only goes to people who asked to be contacted and the system is designed with unsubscribe and suppression built in. It still needs clear protocols and legal groundwork in place before it goes live, so that Digital Collective, its creators, the developer and the people receiving messages are all protected:

  • Approved templates, with correct sender, subject and contact details in every message
  • Recorded consent and a working unsubscribe that is honored promptly
  • Test sends and a full review before email is turned on for real contacts
  • Written agreement on who approves message content and who is responsible for email practices, with legal review of the consent and email wording

Digital Collective is responsible for its own legal review and decisions: the Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, consent wording, Statement of Faith, email practices and any later SMS program. We recommend having a lawyer review these before launch. Legal review is not part of the three payments.
